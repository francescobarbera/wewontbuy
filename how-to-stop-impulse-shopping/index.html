A practical pause

How to stop impulse shopping online

The late-night tab, the sale timer, the cart that already feels like yours. Online shopping is built to make that loop fast: browse, add, pay, done. If you are trying to stop impulse shopping online, you are usually fighting the ritual as much as the item.

You do not have to delete every app or swear off clothes forever. A few pauses help. And when the urge is really about finishing the loop, a €0 cart can finish it for you.

The urge

Notice the ritual, not just the item

Sometimes the satisfying part is not owning the thing. It is choosing it, adding it to a cart, and closing checkout. The parcel often arrives after the feeling has already gone.

If that sounds familiar, treat the next urge as a ritual to complete somewhere safe, not as a test of willpower in a real store.

What to try

A few practical pauses

These are ordinary habits, not a program:

  1. Wait 24 hours. Put the real item down and come back tomorrow. If you still want it, decide then. We Won't Buy has a Cooling-Off Rack that parks an urge for a day for the same reason.
  2. Take saved cards off shopping sites. One-click checkout is designed to skip the pause. Typing a number back in is often enough time to notice you were only browsing.
  3. Unsubscribe from sale emails and mute shopping notifications. An urge that never arrives is the easiest one to skip.
  4. Give the urge somewhere else to go. Closing the tab helps some people. Others still need to browse, choose, and check out. That is what a fake cart is for.

The €0 cart

How a €0 cart helps

We Won't Buy is a fake fashion shop. You can browse fictional garments, fill a cart, and complete checkout. Nothing is for sale and nothing ships. At the end you get a receipt for the money you did not spend.

There is no account and no cookies. Your cart, receipts, and totals stay in your browser. Clearing site data removes them.

This is not a promise about how much you will save. It is only a place to let the impulse finish at €0. As a simple example, €3 a day is €1,095 a year, and one "just because" buy of €40 a month is €480 a year. Use your own numbers.

When the urge hits, open the €0 cart and close the loop there.

No-buy challenges

If you are in a no-buy challenge

A no-buy week, month, or year still leaves the browsing habit in place. When the urge shows up, use the fake shop instead of a real one. The saved dashboard keeps a local total of money not spent and items left imaginary — a reminder of the loop you finished without a delivery.

A small tool, not a treatment

This is a shopping simulator, not a store.

We Won't Buy is a small harm-reduction tool, not therapy and not a cure for shopping addiction. If shopping feels out of control, talking to a professional can really help.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Will a fake shopping cart stop impulse buying?

It can help when the urge is about the ritual: browsing, choosing, and checking out. It will not work for everyone, and it is not a guarantee. If you still want a real item after a pause, you can decide later, with a clearer head.

Do I need an account to use We Won't Buy?

No. There is no account, no cookies, and no backend. Your cart and receipts stay in your browser.

Is this a treatment for shopping addiction?

No. It is a small harm-reduction tool, not therapy. If shopping feels out of control, talking to a professional can really help.
